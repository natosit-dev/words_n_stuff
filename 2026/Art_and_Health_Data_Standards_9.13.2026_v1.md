# Art and Health Data Standards

**Nat Osit**  
September 13, 2026  
Version 1  
**Status:** Essay / public archive

Some people say that data shouldn't be subjective like art, that it's not a painting. I agree that it shouldn't be ambiguous at point of consumption- interpretation requires expensive human processing and produces wildly different semantic models. It produces sparse coupling between meaning and representation, because there's no defined standard to interpret from.

How a potassium lab value makes me feel every time I encounter it is not a useful metric, and it would be wasteful to keep an ongoing record of my feelings on this or any other potato related lab panels.

But representations do not fall from the sky. They have to be materialized, and their existence has to be recorded somewhere using physics- written words, brain zaps, magnets, punch cards, LLMs, etc... we've developed lots of storage media. For storage media to be useful, humans have to supply them with meaning.

Art supplies meaning. It gives structure to raw creativity. Language is an artistic medium, and it's how we materialize human meaning at scale. In healthcare, language is standardized because we're dealing with health and well-being of the world- this is not an organization based on vibes. Evidence and cost are domains most industries are based on. But in healthcare there are many additional domains, and they are as serious as life and death- clinical safety, patient empowerment, population health, social determinants of health.

So to me, I'm not attending an industry networking event where I go listen to people tell me definitions I could have found online. I'm going because I want to learn how healthcare reality is transformed into materialized data infrastructure. I see 2 tracks- those who are translating meaning and relationships into representation, and those who are materializing and transforming that into data and figuring out how to get it to where it will help patients.

That's why PIQI is so interesting to me, because I'm realizing it's asking the question that's I've been obsessed with lately- what survives transformation?

I'm excited to meet other artist engineers and to learn how to build a shared understanding based on patient care! 😁
