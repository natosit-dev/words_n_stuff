# Teacher Recommendation for College

**Nate Osit**  
English 3 AE  
Spring 2003  
Version 1  
**Status:** Historical educational artifact / self-assessment

> **Historical note:** This self-assessment was completed in spring 2003 during the author’s combined junior and senior year of high school. The author did not skip a grade; junior- and senior-year coursework were completed in parallel. Original spelling, grammar, punctuation, and wording are preserved.

1. Significant learning experiences? The concept of a Socratic seminar was new to me, and very helpful. Also going into great depth in class.

2. Words to describe yourself as a participant? Um, Awesome? Curious, enthusiastic, dare I say…insightful? Questioning???

3. English Strengths? Finding symbolism and meaning, writing with good ideas in mind. Problem Areas? Some grammer problems, and ideas occasionally jumbled. Improvements? I think I became a much more analytical writer this year, and my grammer has improved marginally! I now read things more carefully and go in-depth on everything.

4. 2 best analytical papers this year?  
Solid Objects/ Brave New World; Comparing and Contrasting: The excitement that is. Thesis “Pieces of literature that are seemingly unrelated can be very similar when further analyzed” Structure statement “Both of these pieces have very similar themes in them, such as valuing truth over happiness, rejection of common values, and alienation from society.” Although this paper was a bit mixed up in places, I thought I had good ideas about the two stories. Sadly, I don’t think I was able to fully express them because I only used one quote, leaving the reader dumbfounded and me with a B.

Death Remembered: “Wreaths” by Michael Longley. Thesis-The best sort of writing is accomplished through the use of various techniques that imply more than one meaning. Structure-“ It is divided into three sections, each with their own individual meaning implied within.” In the writing of this paper I learned how to look at things closely, and to extract deeper meaning from literature and poetry.

5. Best Tests? Well, I’ve lost most of my tests, but I will assume that I got better grades on books that I took notes on. I bet my Beowulf test was pretty rockin

6. I generally tried pretty hard to participate a lot in class, both when I had something important to say and when I had to inform the class of how much or little something rocked.

7. Peer editor? Cite examples of your Editing skills IN ACTION! – Well, I don’t really have any specific examples, but I think I did my best to point everyone’s errors, although I don’t think I’m the best grammatical editor. But I gave people good ideas to add to papers and to make things more coherent.

8. What books/works excited you this year? Well, I really enjoyed everything we did this year,and Beowulf stands out because of Tim Murphy’s visit. It was very interesting and fun going into the book in excruciating detail. But I’d have to say my favorite book from this year was Brave New World. I really liked that book, and the arguments it brought up about religion, sex, government, drugs, and society in general. I’ve actually ordered some more Aldous Huxley books just yesterday!

9. Favorite assignment? I liked the pilgrim assignment, because it gave us a chance to do some creative writing, and me a chance to write about Robots!

10. If I were to take this course again, I would probably try to read books a little more carefully, and really get into everything we were doing. English is a lot easier when I’m really psyched about it. My grades are generally more reflective of my grammer ability than my overall writing ability, I think. But I think it’s fairly accurate overall. But I don’t think I had enough time this year to dedicate to the class, so that is also reflected.

11. I think I have greatly improved this year as an English student. Seeing as this is kind of my first and last year of real high school English, I have taken away many good lessons and ideas. (Having failed English I and taken English II at home) In the beginning of the year I was very fuzzy on how to write an analytical paper, but very quickly learned how to write one and write a good one. Between English 3 and 4, I have learned a great deal about writing and English in general, and hope to keep improving in college.

12. I feel I best learn when going over worksheets in class. When I take notes on my own I am sometimes unclear as to what to record, but worksheets serve as a kind of funnel through which relevant information can pass. And when we go over it in class it’s interesting to hear what people have written as opposed to the right answer.

13. Were you challenged in this class? Constantly. But it was awesome! Whenever I wrote something, said something, or even thought something it was always like “MORE! More depth and information!” At first I thought Mrs.Anthony was crazy, but after I wrote some awesome papers I started to realize whatever she was doing, it was working. I am now very proud of my papers.

14. Excess Information- Well, most of my free time is spent either playing guitar in jazz band, singing acapella, playing bass and guitar in my own group, or reading. I’m hoping to major in Music Business or Sound Technology at Umass Lowell next year, then go to Berklee. Hmm… I’ve got extra space. Well, this year was totally Gnarly! English 3 was by far my favorite class.
